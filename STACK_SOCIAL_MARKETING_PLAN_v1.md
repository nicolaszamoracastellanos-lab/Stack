## Campaign Overview

**Campaign name:** Founding Crews (ES: Crews Fundadoras)

**One-line summary:** A pre-launch campaign that recruits whole friend groups, not individual signups, so that Stack launches into crews that are already formed rather than into a room of strangers.

**Primary objective:** 25 founding crews committed by launch, where a crew is 3 to 8 named people with one captain. Success is measured in crews, not emails.

**Secondary objectives:**
1. Build a repeatable TikTok production habit that survives launch (the content engine matters more than any single video).
2. Establish Stack's point of view publicly: consistency is a group project, not a personal-discipline problem.
3. Generate real qualitative feedback on the redesigned Home, Check-in, and Landing screens before production merge.
4. Bank a bilingual EN/ES content library that can be reused for App Store screenshots, the Higgsfield promo, and paid tests later.

**The strategic constraint to respect:**

Social media cannot form a group. It can create individual desire, and an individual who downloads Stack alone gets a dead product: no feed, no leaderboard, no nudges, nothing. This is the cold-start problem the pre-dev research named as make-or-break, and no amount of reach solves it.

What has to close the gap:
- **A crew capture mechanic, not an email capture mechanic.** The waitlist form asks for a captain plus 3 to 7 people they will actually bring. One record, many humans.
- **A captain onboarding sequence over Resend** (Hostinger domain, DKIM/SPF/MX already verified) that keeps captains warm and asks them to confirm their crew list before launch week.
- **Direct one-to-one recruiting by Nico.** Social warms, DMs close. Assume roughly half of the 25 crews come from people Nico already knows or messages personally. That is not a failure of the campaign, it is the correct use of it.

**What this campaign is not:** There is no paid tier, no purchase, no client conversion. Do not run "sign up now" creative. The app is not shipped. Any copy implying a live download is a credibility leak.

## Target Audience

### Primary: The Crew Captain
The person in the group chat who already organizes. They send the "gym at 6?" message and get left on read.

- **Profile:** 24 to 35, trains 3 to 5 times a week or wants to, already has a WhatsApp or iMessage group with the exact people Stack needs. Bilingual EN/ES skews high in this cohort for Stack specifically (Chicago plus Latin America roots).
- **Pain points:** Their consistency is fine when someone is watching and collapses when nobody is. They have tried Strava (feels public and endurance-coded), a shared Notes list, and a group chat that died in nine days.
- **Adoption stage:** Problem-aware, solution-unaware. They do not know a private-group accountability app exists. They are not shopping.
- **Where they actually are:** TikTok (fitness, gym humor, discipline content), WhatsApp and iMessage group chats, Instagram Reels. Almost never LinkedIn for this.

### Secondary: The Crew Member
Joins because the captain asked, not because they saw an ad.

- **Profile:** 22 to 40, mixed training level, includes the "I need to start again" segment. Will not download an app on their own initiative.
- **Pain points:** Guilt, restart cycles, feeling behind. Does not want their gym life on a public profile.
- **Adoption stage:** Not in market. Converted by social pressure from a person they trust.
- **Where they actually are:** Wherever the captain sends the link. Marketing reaches them almost entirely secondhand, which is why crew-unit capture matters more than reach.

### Tertiary: The Build-in-Public Audience
Indie hackers, solo founders, GTM and AI-automation people. They will mostly never use Stack.

- **Profile:** Overlaps heavily with Nico's existing professional identity.
- **Pain points:** None that Stack solves. They are here for the build.
- **Value to the campaign:** Amplification, honest product critique, early distribution introductions, and a reputational asset that outlives Stack. They are not a user acquisition channel and should not be modeled as one.
- **Where they actually are:** Threads first, X second, LinkedIn a distant third.

**Why TikTok first:** Three reasons, in order.
1. **The habit already exists.** There is a working TikTok production routine with a partner. The single highest-leverage move in any content plan is feeding the engine that already turns, not starting a second one from cold.
2. **Stack is a consumer app.** The buyer is a 26-year-old who wants their friends to stop flaking. That person is on TikTok and is not reading LinkedIn posts about retention curves.
3. **TikTok still distributes to strangers.** Instagram mostly redistributes to people who already follow you. For a pre-launch product with no audience, algorithmic reach to cold viewers is the entire point. Instagram is where warm people confirm you are real. TikTok is where new crews come from.

Threads sits second, ahead of LinkedIn, because build-in-public content performs natively there, it costs almost nothing to produce, and the audience actively rewards specificity about a product that does not exist yet.

## What We Post

Five pillars. Percentages are of total output across all channels.

### Pillar 1: Proof of Build (30%)
The strongest asset is that Stack is visibly being built by one person with a deadline.

1. "Day X of building an app so my friends stop flaking" running series, one shot of the actual screen, one sentence of what shipped.
2. The redesign side by side: v1 Home versus the v3 prototype Home, same phone, same lighting.
3. The 3D extruded "STACK." wordmark landing screen, touch-only parallax, filmed in hand.
4. "I killed a feature today" post about pushing back on public feeds and follower systems, with the reason.
5. Notion Mission Control tour: the build tracker, the Do now view, the Now/Next/Later roadmap.
6. The Nov 27 countdown as a recurring beat, tied to Nico's own goal, not to a launch promise.
7. "This bug reset everyone's streak" story, and the root fix. Failure content outperforms launch content.

### Pillar 2: The Belief (25%)
The point of view. This is where the crew captain self-identifies.

1. "Your group chat is not a gym plan." The 47-message thread that produced zero workouts.
2. "Discipline is not the problem, being unwatched is." Direct to camera, 15 seconds.
3. The case against public fitness feeds: performance versus accountability. This is Stack's actual thesis.
4. "Why streaks should be weekly, not daily," with the real quota logic. Daily streaks punish real life.
5. ES-native version: "La constancia no es disciplina, es que alguien te esté viendo."
6. Instagram Post 2 (belief carousel, already planned) becomes the anchor piece here. Cut it to a Reel and a TikTok as well, do not let it live only as a carousel.
7. "Nobody has ever gotten in shape alone. They just tell you they did."

### Pillar 3: Crew Stories (20%) · consent required
Oscar, Oski, and the test groups are the origin proof. This is the highest-trust content available and it is entirely gated on them saying yes.

1. "The first three people who used this app were my brothers." Origin story, direct to camera.
2. Oscar and Oski reacting to their own streaks, filmed casually, phones visible.
3. A real crew's week in the app, sanitized: check-ins, reactions, the at-risk nudge landing.
4. "What my test group told me to fix" and the change that resulted.
5. The Ctoma group's ES-language check-ins as a bilingual proof point (only with explicit consent, group names may need to be renamed on screen).
6. A short interview: "Why did you actually keep going?"

**Decision to confirm, do not assume:** Ask Oscar, Oski, and each test group individually whether they are comfortable appearing by first name, by face, or not at all. Give them a clean "no" option and a "blurred and renamed" option. Do not post a single frame of their data or faces before an explicit yes.

### Pillar 4: Product Glimpse (15%)
Micro-demos. Real footage only, never AI-generated UI.

1. The consistency ring filling from 0 to 50%, single unbroken screen recording.
2. The at-risk streak alert firing. The only red in the product, and it lands hard on camera.
3. The Prove it flow end to end: sport picker, group picker, photo, watermark, posted.
4. The shareable card generating.
5. Group pacts and honor-system stakes, framed as "put something on the line with people who know you."
6. The EN/ES toggle flipping the entire interface mid-video. This one does numbers with a bilingual audience.
7. Rest days: "the app tells you it is fine to rest, and the streak survives."

### Pillar 5: Bilingual and Culture (10%)
1. "Stackear" as a verb explainer. Naming a behavior is how it spreads.
2. Latin American gym culture versus US gym culture, played for comedy, landing on the same insight.
3. Full Spanish version of the belief video, not subtitled English.
4. Chicago-specific: winter, the January crowd, February emptiness, and what actually predicts who is still there in March.

## How We Post

### Format mix
- 70% vertical video, 9:16, 7 to 25 seconds, hook in the first 1.5 seconds
- 15% screen recording with voiceover, 20 to 45 seconds
- 10% text-only (Threads)
- 5% static or carousel (Instagram only, mostly the belief carousel and brand cards)

### Weekly production system (2 hours total, hard cap)

Stack has two active marketing tracks (TikTok as the engine, Instagram pre-launch as the finishing sequence) and one much bigger track that is the actual product. Marketing gets two hours a week and no more. If it needs more, cut scope, not build time.

| Block | When | Length | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| Capture | Rolling, in the gym and at the desk | 0 min budgeted | Film raw clips as they happen. Phone, no setup. Never schedule this. |
| Batch session | Sunday | 75 min | Record 4 to 6 TikToks with partner, cut, caption EN/ES, schedule. See Content Generation Guide for the exact run of show. |
| Screen capture | Sunday, inside batch | 15 min | Fresh screen recordings from the live build. Sanitize immediately. |
| Threads drip | Weekdays, in gaps | 15 min/week | 1 post per weekday, written from what actually happened in the build that day. No batching, it kills the voice. |
| Review | Friday | 15 min | Pull numbers, log to Notion Build Tracker, decide next week's one experiment. |

### Posting mechanics
- Post TikTok natively, never cross-posted with a watermark
- 12:00 to 13:00 or 19:00 to 21:00 CT, test both for two weeks then stop testing
- Caption in the dominant language of the video, first comment in the other language
- Every post ends with the same spoken or written line: "Link in bio if you want your crew in the first group."
- No hashtag spam. Three to five, mixed EN/ES
- Reply to every comment for the first 60 minutes. This is the single highest-ROI 60 minutes in the plan

### The crew capture loop (Stack's conversion mechanic)

This replaces a client-conversion DM loop. There is nothing to buy, so the ask is a commitment of people, not money.

1. **Hook in content:** "Comment CREW and I will send you the link" or "Link in bio."
2. **Reply:** Send stack-app.online. Personal reply, not a bot.
3. **Landing page captures a crew, not an email.** Fields: captain first name, email, "how many people are in your crew" (3 to 8), and optionally the first names of who they are bringing. Naming the people is the commitment device, and it also gives us expected crew size for the retention model.
4. **Immediate Resend confirmation** (EN or ES based on toggle): "You are captain of crew #N. Bring 3 to 7 people. We will tell you when it is live."
5. **Drip, every 10 to 14 days:** one build update, one belief piece, one "confirm your crew" ask. Short. No newsletter formatting.
6. **Launch week:** captains get access first, plus an invite code for their crew, and a single instruction: get 3 people to check in within 48 hours.
7. **DM close for high-intent commenters.** If someone comments something real, message them directly and ask who their three people are. This is how at least half of the 25 crews will actually be built.

**Dependency:** step 3 requires a form on stack-app.online wired to Supabase or Resend. If the landing page is currently static, this is the single highest-priority build item in the entire plan, ahead of any content.

## Success Metrics

The trap here is measuring a waitlist funnel that ends at "signups." Stack's north star is Week-4 group retention, and a waitlist full of individuals who cannot form a crew produces zero retained groups. So every metric ladders to crews.

### 30-day targets

| Metric | Target | How it is tracked |
|---|---|---|
| Crew captains registered | 40 | Supabase waitlist table, count of records with crew_size ≥ 3 |
| Average declared crew size | ≥ 3.5 | Supabase, mean of crew_size |
| Total humans committed (captains × declared crew) | 140 | Derived |
| TikToks shipped | 18 to 20 | Manual count in Notion Build Tracker |
| Threads posts shipped | 20 | Manual count |
| Instagram Posts 2 and 3 | Both live | Binary |
| Median TikTok view count | Establish baseline, no target | TikTok analytics. Month 1 sets the benchmark, do not chase a number you have not measured yet |
| Comment-to-DM conversations started | 25 | Manual tally |
| Captain email open rate | ≥ 55% | Resend |

### 60-day targets

| Metric | Target | How it is tracked |
|---|---|---|
| Crew captains registered | 90 | Supabase |
| **Launch-ready crews** (captain has named 3+ specific people) | **25** | Supabase, count of records with named_members ≥ 3 |
| Captains who reply to the "confirm your crew" email | ≥ 40% | Resend plus Supabase |
| Crews that complete onboarding within 72h of access | ≥ 60% of invited crews | Supabase |
| **Week-4 group retention, first founding cohort** | **≥ 40% of activated crews** | `group_weekly_retention` view (Appendix A v1.1 of the master plan), Monday to Sunday local time |

**On the retention target:** category benchmark for health and fitness apps is roughly 3% Day-30 retention at the individual level. The master plan's thesis is that a closed group retains at a multiple of that. 40% of activated crews still showing group-level activity in Week 4 is the number that would validate the entire product thesis. It is aggressive on purpose. If the first cohort lands under 15%, the problem is the product loop, not the marketing, and content spend should stop until that is fixed.

**Definition to lock before launch:** a crew counts as retained in Week 4 if at least 50% of its members posted at least one check-in during that Monday to Sunday window. This definition needs to match the `group_weekly_retention` view exactly. If the view uses a different rule, the view wins and this document gets updated, not the other way around.

### Reporting cadence
- **Friday, 15 minutes:** log content counts, captain count, and crew count to the Notion Build Tracker. Pick one experiment for next week.
- **Monthly:** roll up into the Master Execution Business Plan against the 90-day phase gates.
- **Post-launch, weekly:** Week-4 group retention becomes the top line of the report and everything above it becomes supporting detail.

| **HealthKit privacy policy copy (EN + ES) not written** | High | Hard dependency for both App Store and Health Connect review. Until it exists, no marketing claim about health-verified check-ins, Apple Health, or automatic workout detection. Treat that entire feature as non-existent publicly. |
| **Capacitor timeline unknown** | Medium | Do not tie any campaign beat to a native release date. All calendar items above are web-safe. |
| **Higgsfield promo blocked on audio** | Medium | Week 4 slot has a documented fallback: a 15-second silent typographic cut. Decide at day 22, not day 28. |
| **Consent from Oscar, Oski, and test groups** | Medium | Ask explicitly, offer face-blur and rename options, accept no. Two calendar slots have written fallbacks. Never post a frame before a yes. |
| **Real user data leaks in screen recordings** | High | Sanitize checklist in the Content Generation Guide is mandatory. Current screens show real names, real avatars, real group names, and at least one group description that should never be public. |
| **Top-tier streak tier name exposed** | Medium | Explicit frame-by-frame check on every UI clip. It is never shown in user-facing surfaces and it must never appear in marketing either. |
| **Red used outside at-risk alerts** | Medium | Locked in the brand block. No red CTAs, no red accents, no red thumbnails. Volt is the only accent. |
| **Single-partner dependency on TikTok** | Medium | The channel is only as reliable as the partner's availability. Bank a 2-week buffer of edited clips. Keep at least one format Nico can shoot solo (screen recording plus voiceover). |
| **Content time crowds out build time** | High | Hard 2-hour weekly cap. Stack's standing risk is over-building and over-producing instead of recruiting real groups. If a week forces a choice, message three real people instead of making a fourth video. |
| **Going semi-viral with nothing to give people** | Medium | The crew capture form plus the Resend sequence is the catch net. Do not push for reach before the net exists. |
| **Copy drift: em dashes, English-first Spanish, machine translation** | Low | Brand block enforced on every generation. Spanish is written, never translated. |
